# Blocks & Ledgers — treatment

Style: Whiteboard Explainer (`whiteboard`). Source: "Basics with Block: Blocks and Distributed Ledgers" (blackboardblock, Medium, 8 May 2025), the user's own article and character.
Brief: English · **no voice, no subtitles** (the handwriting on the board carries every word) · vertical 1080×1920, 24 fps · ~52 s · music + foley · −14 LUFS · no storyboard stop.

## 1. Three structures

- **A. The recipe card (chosen).** The board is a tall recipe card read top to bottom. The article's cake recipe is written ingredient by ingredient, baked into a block, and the blocks chain downward. The vertical frame makes "a chain grows" literal: the camera rides the chain down. Then a ledger, its copies, one cheat, and the sum.
- **B. Block's journey from the future.** A timeline from 2060 back to today, Block visiting each concept. Fun, but spends the 50 s on travel instead of the two ideas.
- **C. The debate.** One keeper's book on the left half vs. a shared book on the right. Clear for the ledger part, but the block/recipe half has nothing to argue against.

A wins: the article's strongest image is the recipe, and a recipe *is* "writing in stroke order" (the style's first native move). Opening: the claim "A BLOCK IS A CAKE." Ending: the sum, circled twice, the marker capped, Block waves.

## 2. Logline and arc

Block teaches that a blockchain is just a baked recipe, chained, and copied to everyone, so one cheater can't change it.
Setup: a block is a cake (recipe) → Turn: one person edits their copy of the ledger and everyone else's copy outvotes it → Ending: Blocks + Distributed Ledger = Blockchain.

## 3. Benchmark

- **Vi Hart's doodle videos.** Learn: the pen as the narrator, drawings that grow out of the previous drawing, fast but never losing stroke order. Don't take: her voice, drawings, music.
- **Bake-off style recipe cards (TV baking shows).** Learn: the ingredient rhythm (quantity, ingredient, little drawing, next line), the "into the oven" beat as a pause. Don't take: any show's logo, type, set or music.

## 4. Shot list (1080×1920; board 4000 × 7600 board px)

Revised after the demo comparison (DIRECTOR.md §11): the **signature shot is the vertical track down the chain**, riding the pen's arrow from block to block; the film **never pulls back past the board edge** (that was the demo's ending). Board map: hook and recipe card at the top of the left column, the chain running down it, the ledger and its copies on the right, the equation at the bottom left, under the chain.

## 6. Cue map

100 BPM, 4/4, 1 bar = 2.4 s; 22 bars (52.8 s). Key F major; the ledger section moves to B♭ (a new idea). All plucked and synthesized: `core/audio/pluck.py` **kalimba**, **music_box** and **upright_bass_pizz**, plus a **synthesized shaker** (filtered noise bursts). No marimba, woodblock or glockenspiel (that was the demo's clockwork palette).

## 7. Sound design

| Section | Ambience | Foley | Music |
|---|---|---|---|
| hook | low room tone | fast marker strokes (hiss + body), cap off | kalimba |
| recipe | room tone | marker per line, one squeak on "Bake it." | builds 1 instrument per line |
| bake | room tone | hatch scribble, **ding**, magnet click | + shaker, chord + kalimba run |
| chain | room tone | long stroke for the arrow, magnet clicks (metal + board thump) | full groove |
| ledger | room tone | whoosh on the whip, marker | thinner, new key |
| copies | room tone | quick strokes ×8 | music-box arpeggio |
| cheat | room tone only in the silence | red marker squeak, green ticks, eraser felt rub | silence → pings → reversed bar |
| sum | room tone | long writing, two circle strokes | full |
| end | room tone | cap on click | ring-out |

Two silences: 36.0–36.8 (before the cheat) and the tail after 51.6. Sound as transition: the whip's whoosh starts before the camera leaves the chain (J-cut); the ding rings over the first chain arrow (L-cut).

## 8. Titles and type

No voice → **no subtitles**; every word is written on the board in one single-line hand, stroke order, held long enough to read (`readcheck`). Colours: black `#23262c` for things and structure, green `#2e8b57` for agreement (the shared copies, ticks, "BLOCKCHAIN"), red `#c8363a` only for the cheat (one accent per region). Titles ~150 px cap height on screen, labels ≥ 56 px so they read on a phone.
Text taken from the article (shortened to labels where needed): "1 cup … transactions", "1/2 cup timestamp", "1 cup of previous block", "Bake it", "locks it in place", "Blocks + Distributed Ledger = Blockchain". The record-book names (Ana, Ben, Cem) are invented examples.
